## Objective

`vpn.NewPlaneHost` (`pkg/sdn/gateway/services/vpn/datapath_host.go`) and
`ipc.Server.SetVPNPlanes` (`pkg/sdn/gateway/ipc/server.go`) have no
production caller: the datapath child served by
`pkg/sdn/gateway/commands/service.go` answers every `InstallVPNPeer` with the
`noVPNPlanes` refusal. This row wires the VPN planes into the datapath child
of gatewayd so the peer RPCs reach a real plane.

## Lifecycle: peer expiry, IPAM ownership, restart (ruling 931)

### Who owns peer expiry

The VPN service in the gatewayd control process owns it. `vpn.Service` runs the expiry sweep (`sweepLoop`, every `expirySweep`): `Sweep` revokes each lease past its expiry and has the datapath child remove its peer. The datapath child keeps no deadlines; it programs the peers it is told to.

`Stop` leaves leases installed, because a restart of the control process must not drop admitted clients. While the expiry authority is down, an expired peer keeps its session until the rebuilt projection sweeps again.

Open, as owner queue item 237 (Codex, `pkg/sdn/gateway/process/supervisor.go:911`):
- **Recommended:** the datapath enforces each peer's lease deadline itself. The deadline travels with `InstallVPNPeer`, so expiry holds while the control process is down.
- **The alternative:** withdraw every peer when the expiry authority dies.

### IPAM ownership per gateway

Each VPN address allocation in the lease pool carries the `vpn` tag and the owner tag `gateway:<id>`. The ID is the control-plane reporter's (`Reporter.GatewayID`).

A gateway reclaims only allocations that carry its exact owner tag and that neither a stored lease nor an owed peer removal holds.

A gateway with no control plane has no owner tag, so it reclaims nothing: orphan reclaim is off there (accepted, ruling 931). An allocation with no owner tag, including any written before owner tags existed, is never reclaimed. There is no backfill.

### Restart sequence

1. **Datapath child crash.** The supervisor relaunches the child with backoff (`DefaultRestartBackoff`, doubling up to `DefaultRestartBackoffMax`). The relaunched child sweeps its predecessor's flows on start.
2. **Follower given up.** The supervisor registers the peer withdrawal under the same lock that decides the give-up, and only when no replacement launch is claimed. A relaunch waits for that withdrawal to finish before it execs the replacement. The withdrawal checks for a relaunch before every round, including the first.
3. **Control process restart.** The lease projection is rebuilt from the control plane. The admission journal wins over the control plane: a lease it names is revoked and kept out of the projection.
4. **`Start`.** The expiry sweep resumes.
5. **`Restore`.** Each stored lease takes its address back and has its peer programmed. A lease that has expired, or whose plane, zones or address the gateway can no longer give it, is dropped and reported as `ErrLeaseNotRestored`.
6. **Orphan reclaim.** Once every plane matches the stored leases, the gateway's own unheld allocations are released. A failed reclaim stays owed (`ErrOrphanReclaimFailed`) and a later `Sweep` retries it.
7. **Enrolment set.** Applying the set sweeps out restored leases of peers that the control plane withdrew while the gateway was down.

## Design: WireGuard as its own gatewayd service process (ruling 942)

Replaces slices 1–1i. Owner decision (item 237): WireGuard runs on gatewayd as its own service, not in the platformd control plane or the datapath binary. It can be stopped and restarted independently of the main server, and its crash does not take down the gateway. Cites are on the #1717 head (`pr/1717`).

1. **Process boundary.** A new process-table row `wireguard` (process/class.go, next to `datapath`):
   - ClassKernel, with CAP_NET_ADMIN only and AF_UNIX/AF_NETLINK only. The kernel module binds the UDP port, so the process opens no inet socket.
   - Supervised, with a WatchdogInterval set. It runs a new service constant, `gateway.ServiceVPNWireGuard`, enabled when management_vpn is enabled and offers WireGuard (non-FIPS only).
   - The same gatewayd binary, started as `gatewayd service run wireguard` (process/launcher.go:141). It is not part of platformd or of the datapath child.
   - On its own supervisor socket it serves ProcessControlService and a new VPNPlaneService. VPNPlaneService takes InstallVPNPeer, RemoveVPNPeer and ReconcileVPNPeers out of DatapathService (ipc.proto:44-50) and adds RenewVPNPeer. The datapath child stops answering these verbs.
2. **Device and peer ownership.** Only the `wireguard` process creates, programs and deletes the hub device:
   - **Start:** `WireGuardHub.Up` deletes any stale device, then creates a fresh one (tunnel/wireguard_hub.go:121,178).
   - **Graceful stop:** `Down` deletes the device and every peer on it (services/vpn/dataplane_wireguard.go:107).
   - Peers are held only in that process's memory. The supervisor may delete the device when the process exits (point 4), but it never programs it.
   - `removeStaleVPNHub` leaves the datapath start path (commands/service.go:148).
3. **Peer lease expiry.** The `wireguard` process enforces each peer's deadline itself:
   - VPNPeer gains `expires_at`. The plane removes a peer when its deadline passes and tombstones it, so a late install cannot land (dataplane_wireguard.go:122). The vpn child is not involved.
   - **Renewal:** RenewVPNPeer can only extend a peer the plane already holds. If it fails, the old deadline stands (fail closed), and the retry is owed to Sweep (restore.go:227).
   - The vpn child's sweep still revokes the record and releases the address (service.go:251-268).
4. **Fail closed.**
   - **Crash, SIGKILL or a missed watchdog:** a new supervisor `OnChildExit` hook in `watch` (supervisor.go:889) runs the deletion-only `tunnel.RemoveStaleWireGuardHub` (tunnel/wireguard_stale.go:33), so the device and all its peers are gone; then backoff and relaunch. `Up` starts with an empty device, and peers return by replay. A failed deletion is logged at Error, reported in health, and retried on every backoff tick; the relaunch's `Up` deletes the device first anyway. Once the restart budget is spent, the device stays absent and WireGuard admissions are refused UNAVAILABLE. MASQUE is unaffected.
   - **Operator stop:** SIGTERM, then `Down`. Past StopTimeout: SIGKILL, then the exit hook. A start behaves like a relaunch.
   - **gatewayd serve dies:** the children are killed (launcher.go:244). The gatewayd service unit removes the stale hub on every stop (ExecStopPost) and `serve` deletes any stale hub before every start, so a dead serve never leaves peers installed (Q3; tested).
   - **vpn child crash:** peers stay, each bounded by its own deadline. Restore runs ReconcileVPNPeers and then reinstalls them (services/vpn/restore.go:37,83). If the vpn child is given up, the supervisor withdraws every peer (Q5, approved).
   - **datapath crash:** no effect on the device or its peers. `FollowsDatapath` goes away.
   - **Replay after a `wireguard` restart:** the supervisor relaunches the vpn child with #1717's follower code, `Follows: "wireguard"`. Restore reinstalls every unexpired stored lease with its deadline (Q4, approved).
5. **IPAM per gateway** (as ruled in 931, owned by the vpn child): each allocation is tagged `vpn` and `gateway:<id>` (admission.go:539,559), the id being the reporter's GatewayID (controlplane/reporter.go:279). Orphan reclaim covers only exactly-tagged, unheld allocations (restore.go:137); a gateway with no control plane reclaims nothing. The `wireguard` process holds no IPAM and refuses any allowed prefix other than a single host address inside the hub's network.
6. **Supervisor fit.** The same restart policy for every process: 500 ms doubling to 30 s, a budget of 5 in 5 min (supervisor.go:43-52). Each process has its own cgroup leaf, socket and restartLoop. A `wireguard` crash touches only its own entry, the hub device, and the vpn child (through replay); the datapath, routing, proxy and provisioning children and `gatewayd serve` are not touched. #1717's general supervisor fixes stay: `proc.stopping` in watch, and `awaitWanted` after a relaunch.
